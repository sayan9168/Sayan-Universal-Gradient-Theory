# The Sayan Universal Gradient (SUG) Theory — Postulates

> **Status:** SUG is a *proposed* theoretical framework. These postulates are
> axioms the author adopts, not established laws of physics. They are stated
> precisely so that the framework can be criticised, falsified, or refined.

Every theory is a tower built on a few chosen premises.  The **Sayan
Universal Gradient (SUG)** theory begins from five explicit postulates.  They
are deliberately few and intentionally elementary; all later structure is
*derived* from them rather than assumed.

---

## Postulate I — A single scalar field couples three quantities

There exists a scalar field, the **Sayan–Universal field** and is written
:math:`\Theta_S`, that simultaneously encodes the *energy released by a
source*, the *thermal gradient experienced at a point*, and the *distance of
that point from the source*.

**Consequence.** Thermal, energetic and geometric information about a high-
energy event is not three separate book-keeping lists; it is one continuous
field over space.  Where classical thermodynamics treats temperature as a
local state variable and distance as an independent coordinate, SUG proposes
they share a single organising field.

---

## Postulate II — The field decreases with the *logarithm* of distance

The magnitude of :math:`\Theta_S` falls off as :math:`1/\log(x)` with the
dimensionless distance :math:`x = r/r_0`, not as the classical inverse-square
:math:`1/x^2` or inverse-linear :math:`1/x`.

**Consequence.** A logarithm falls far more slowly than any power law.  SUG
therefore predicts that "reach" of a high-energy disturbance in vacuum is far
longer-lived than inverse-square models suggest — the motivation for the
"vacuum destruction radius" being extremely large (see
`formal_framework.md`).

> This is the theory's most aggressive and most testable assumption.  It is
> also the postulate most likely to conflict with the well-established
> inverse-square law of radiative energy spread, and it must be reconciled
> with that law (see `open_questions.md`).

---

## Postulate III — The field grows as the square root of released energy

For a fixed geometry, :math:`\Theta_S \propto \sqrt{E_{nb}}`.  Energy enters
the field through its square root rather than linearly or quadratically.

**Consequence.** There is a *compressive* mapping from energy to field:
doubling released energy raises the field by only :math:`\sqrt{2}\approx
1.41\times`.  This gives the theory a mild saturation behaviour — past a
point, adding energy buys increasingly little extra "field".

---

## Postulate IV — The field is damped inversely by the thermal gradient

:math:`\Theta_S \propto 1/\Delta T`: a larger thermal gradient *opposes* the
field.  Cold points (small :math:`\Delta T` with respect to the regime's
temperature scale) feel a stronger field than warm points.

**Consequence.** The theory contains an explicit *thermal antagonism*:
energy wants to build the field, ambient heat (gradient) wants to dismantle
it.  This single mechanism yields the "thermal mapping" pillar — SUG can say
*where* along a temperature profile destruction is most intense.

---

## Postulate V — A critical threshold separates matter from information/energy

There exists a threshold :math:`\theta_c`, characteristic of the material or
system in question, above which matter can no longer retain its bound state
and converts, in the theory's language, into a pure information/energy
degree of freedom.

**Consequence.** Once :math:`\theta_c` is fixed for a material, the field
inversion produces concrete, geometric predictions: a *destruction radius*
and a *breaking energy* (both derived in `formal_framework.md`).  Without a
threshold the theory only describes a field; with one threshold it describes
*events*.

---

## What these postulates are *not*

- They are not derived from first principles of an established theory.
- They are not claimed to be unique — other fields could be constructed.
- They are axioms chosen for *simplicity and internal consistency*.

Their value stands or falls on whether the *derived predictions* in
`predictions.md` survive comparison with data after the model's parameters
are calibrated.  That is the honest test of any axiomatic framework.
